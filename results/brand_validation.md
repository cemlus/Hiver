plenty for classification and escalation. There are about 4.4k grounding examples, but only
around 100–165 cases where the customer confirmed that the public reply fixed the problem.

- 205 non-English tweets
- 1,267 tweets with fewer than 3 words (bare links, "Yes", "Done")
- 49 "DM sent" hand-off messages

**2. Distinct threads: 12,703** have at least one usable exchange (13,455 contain any
XboxSupport tweet), and 3,556 have a substantive reply. That is about 1.5 usable exchanges per
thread, so Phase 2 must split by thread.

**3. Resolved candidates:**
- **439** exchanges where the customer's next tweet confirms a fix, **165** of them after a
  substantive reply.
- A hand check of 12 `fix_confirmed` cases found all 12 really resolved, but only about 2 credit
  XboxSupport's public reply. Most customers fixed it themselves or via chat or phone.
- Of 8 fix-after-substantive-reply cases, about 4 credit the reply. So roughly **80–100
  exchanges show a public reply that demonstrably worked.**
- `thanks_only` (1,049) means acknowledged, not resolved: about 2 of 12 samples mention a fix.
- 57.7% of exchanges get no customer answer at all.

Implication: weak outcome labels are far too sparse to evaluate against. They can only nudge
retrieval re-ranking. This confirms the plan: evaluate against human golden labels and the judge.

Every draft intent has at least 387 examples, which is enough for few-shot examples, the
knowledge base and a TF-IDF baseline. The pinch is the golden set: in a 120-tweet random slice,
enforcement, code redemption and subscription would get only about 2.5–3.4 examples each.
- The planned 80-tweet stratified slice must top the rare intents up to roughly 10 each.
- Otherwise, merge the purchase, code and subscription intents into one "purchases, codes and
  memberships" intent.

The 41.7% unmatched reflects the keyword rules' low recall, not tweets without an intent. The
samples are mostly vague help requests ("help me please", "it's not working") and context-dependent
follow-ups. The codebook therefore needs a **vague / needs-more-info** intent (reply strategy: ask
for details) and explicit rules for follow-ups. Otherwise `other` will far exceed the plan's
10–15% target.

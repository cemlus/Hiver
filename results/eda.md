## Recommendation: XboxSupport (runner-up: SpotifyCares)

The largest brands are not the best fit. XboxSupport gives the best mix of the five criteria.
All figures come from the tables below.

| Criterion | XboxSupport | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| Sufficient volume | 24,557 brand replies to 20,213 customer tweets, in 13,455 threads | Enough for a training pool of several thousand plus ~240 held-out hand-labelled examples |
| Diverse recurring intents | Connectivity/network, console won't power on or freezes, game-specific bugs, store purchases and payment, code redemption, Live/Game Pass membership, account and sign-in, enforcement and suspensions, achievements, controllers, product questions | Each implies a different reply or escalation policy, which is what the codebook needs |
| Substantive historical resolutions | 20.8% substantive replies, the highest of the top 15. Longest conversations (4.3 tweets on average, p90 = 7). 42.9% of threads get a customer follow-up; 10.1% end on a thanks/fixed signal (both highest) | Real step-by-step troubleshooting, not only "DM us" |
| Useful grounding for RAG | 4,775 distinct substantive replies; 40% of replies carry a link (mostly support articles) | A deep enough pool for k = 5 retrieval |
| Manageable noise | 98.9% English. 99.4% of replies end in an agent tag (`^XS`) and 31% are split across tweets (`1/2`, trailing ` 1`) | Systematic, so simple Phase 2 rules can strip tags and merge split replies |

It also has natural escalation cases: enforcement appeals, account security, purchase and
billing problems, and anything that needs the customer's Gamertag (19% of replies are DM-only).

**Why not the others**

- **AppleSupport** has the most volume, the cleanest formatting (no sign-offs or splits) and the
  largest grounding pool (20,835 distinct substantive replies). But it is dominated by one event:
  the iOS 11 update, battery drain and the "I → A[?]" autocorrect bug. One canned workaround link
  was sent 6,250 times, 45% of replies are DM-only, and the median thread is a single exchange.
  A golden set drawn from it would be dominated by one easy, canned case, which would inflate the
  headline numbers.
- **SpotifyCares** has varied intents (account and login, Premium/Family/Student billing, content
  availability, playlists, playback bugs, feature requests) and little noise. But only 13.0% of
  replies are substantive: many are "DM us your account email" (30.5% DM-only) or "not available
  yet / feedback passed on". It is the fallback if XboxSupport's split replies prove hard to merge.
- **TMobileHelp** is 74% DM-only. A hand check found about 1 in 12 of its "substantive" replies
  actually solves anything; the rest are DM invitations. There's almost nothing to ground on.
- **AmazonHelp** (the largest) is only about 81% English (Spanish, French, Japanese, Portuguese),
  and just 8% of its replies are actionable.
- **Uber_Support, comcastcares and Ask_Spectrum** are 48–70% DM-only with heavy canned replies.
- **Airlines, rail and Tesco** have few actionable replies. Their answers depend on live bookings
  or travel status, which historical replies can't ground.

**Risks to carry into Phase 2**

- XboxSupport is the smallest candidate. That is fine for this plan, but rare intents will be thin
  in the golden set, so the stratified slice matters.
- Split replies must be merged before brand replies are used as grounding or as reference answers.
- About 26% of replies are canned DM templates. Retrieval should down-weight them.
- Like every brand, nearly all activity is in October–November 2017 (99.7% of tweets fall in the
  last 90 days), so the time-based split falls within about two months.

**How far to trust these numbers**

All text metrics are keyword heuristics, spot-checked by hand on random samples:
- DM-only replies and language labels were right in every sample read.
- About 8–9 of 12 "substantive" samples were genuinely substantive for XboxSupport, AppleSupport
  and SpotifyCares, against about 1 of 12 for TMobileHelp.
- About two-thirds of outcome-signal samples were plausible, so they are used for comparison only.
- I also read 30 random threads per candidate, in addition to the appendix samples.

## Dataset facts

- 2,811,774 tweets: 1,537,843 from customers, 1,273,931 from 108 brand accounts.
- Tweet dates: 2008-05-08 to 2017-12-03; 99.7% fall in the final 90 days.
- `response_tweet_id` holds several comma-separated IDs in 222,426 tweets, so threads are trees, not chains. Threads here are rebuilt from `in_response_to_tweet_id` (parent pointers).
- 106,891 tweets contain line breaks inside quoted text, which pyarrow's CSV reader rejects. Load with pandas' default C engine.
- 3,862 tweets (0.1%) reply to a tweet that isn't in the dataset, so some threads start mid-conversation.
- Customer handles are anonymised: 99.7% of brand replies mention a numeric handle such as `@115712`.

## Method and caveats

- **DM redirect**: the reply mentions DM / direct or private message. **DM-only**: a DM redirect with no actionable cue.
- **Actionable**: contains a troubleshooting or guidance cue (restart, update, settings, try, go to, power cycle, …). Keyword-based: it over-counts ("try DMing us") and under-counts (fixes phrased unusually, informational answers such as "this song isn't available yet").
- **Canned**: the normalised reply (no handles, URLs, sign-offs, digits, punctuation) occurs ≥ 5 times for that brand.
- **Sign-off**: an agent tag at the end of the reply (`^JK`, `*RickK`, `/NS`, `-Sam`). **Split reply**: one answer spread over several tweets (`1/2`, a leading `2:`, a trailing ` 1`).
- **English share**: counts English vs Spanish/French/German/Portuguese/Dutch function words and checks for non-Latin scripts. Tweets with neither are `undetermined` and excluded from the share.
- **Outcome signals** are weak heuristics, used here only to compare brands. They are never ground truth.
